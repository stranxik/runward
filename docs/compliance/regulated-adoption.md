# Adopting runward in a regulated environment

> **Scope of this sheet.** runward 0.33.5, revised 2026-08-12. Every statement below about the gate's behaviour is a statement about this build and no other; earlier builds behaved differently on at least one point recorded here (section 5). Section 5 carries the defects found in this build, the measured limits of the test suite that guards it, and the results that count against us. Section 8 carries the tool confidence analysis, written adverse case first. Nothing on this page is a qualification, a certification, or a confidence class. Those are determined in your environment, against your own criticality argument, and no external body has assessed this project.

runward is a **local, open-source CLI**. It runs inside your repository, emits no data, hosts nothing, and makes no network call. So most vendor due-diligence — which is built to assess a third party that *holds your data* — does not apply. What remains is **artifact integrity**, **project health**, and a third axis most vendor questionnaires do not carry, because they were not written for a tool that renders a verdict: **whether the verdict is right**. runward decides pass or fail on your evidence, so its characteristic failure mode is a green granted wrongly, that is, a failure to detect. If your process classifies development tools before use, that is the axis those schemes weigh most heavily, and section 5 states what has been measured on it. Section 8 works the analysis through for medical device, automotive, rail and airborne software, starting from the arguments an assessor would use against us. Hand this page to your TPRM / security / procurement team.

> **Posture in one line.** runward produces deterministic, replayable *engineering evidence* that **feeds** your compliance programme (ISO/IEC 42001, NIST AI RMF, the EU AI Act technical documentation (art. 11 / Annex IV)). It is not a compliance validator, and it never claims to be. See [ADR-0031](../adr/ADR-0031-sovereign-engineering-evidence-for-regulated-environments.md).

## 1. What does NOT apply (because runward is local, not a SaaS)

| Control usually demanded of a vendor | Why it is moot here |
|---|---|
| SOC 2 / ISO 27001 of a hosting provider | No host. runward runs in your repo, on your infrastructure. |
| DPA / GDPR art. 28 (processor agreement) | No third party in the data flow: runward runs on your infrastructure, so there is no processor relationship to govern. (Your repo may contain personal data — but it stays with you, under your existing controls.) |
| Data residency / sovereign hosting | Nothing is hosted or transmitted. Your repo, your region. |
| Penetration test of the service | No service is exposed — there is no runtime, no endpoint. |
| Sub-processors list | None. |
| Encryption in transit / at rest (vendor side) | No vendor side. Your existing repo controls apply. |
| Vendor business-continuity / exit plan | MIT-licensed and forkable; no hosted dependency to lose. |

The reason is structural: these controls evaluate a third party in the **data flow**, and runward is not in that flow, so they answer nothing here. State that on the questionnaire, with the reason, rather than leaving the cell blank. Do not read it as a security argument: taking the vendor out removes one class of risk and leaves another that a SaaS questionnaire never asks about, code executing inside your repository under your CI credentials. Section 5.1 records a defect of exactly that shape, a symlink escaping containment and turning the seal into an arbitrary file read (RWD-2026-0005).

## 3. OpenSSF OSPS Baseline alignment

Assessed against the **OpenSSF OSPS Baseline, version 2026-02-19**, the edition in force on 2026-08-06. Read the level first, because it changes what the family names mean. The Baseline scopes Level 2 to projects with at least two maintainers; runward has one, which places it at Level 1. At Level 1 the Baseline carries no automated testing control at all: OSPS-QA-06 applies at Levels 2 and 3, OSPS-QA-07 at Level 3 only. So the quality line below is information about this project, not evidence of a control the Baseline requires of it at its level.

This is a self-assessment by the maintainer, control family by control family, not a filled per-control attestation and not an external review. What follows is the material a reviewer would check.

- **Build & release:** signed provenance (SLSA), SBOM, SHA-pinned actions, published integrity metadata.
- **Vulnerability management & disclosure:** `SECURITY.md` with a private reporting channel and coordinated disclosure.
- **Access & governance:** `CODEOWNERS` and review-by-default; no committed secrets; **no long-lived Actions secrets** at all — publishing uses OIDC trusted publishing, so there is no `NPM_TOKEN` or PAT to leak.
- **Quality:** the test suite is deterministic and, for the offline job, network-isolated; the emitted OSCAL is golden-tested. Those are properties of how the suite runs. What it *catches* is a separate question, it has been measured, and the number is unfavourable: see section 5.2.

## 4. Licence framing

- **Tooling — MIT.** Freely reusable, no copyleft, no viral obligation. The favourable case in a regulated review.
- **Doctrine / method — CC BY-ND 4.0.** You may **use and apply** it internally: since CC 4.0, a *private* adaptation that is not shared is permitted. What the ND clause forbids is **redistributing a modified version** of the canon. Internal use therefore creates no distributed derivative and raises no obligation; only re-publishing an altered doctrine would.

## 5. What runward is NOT — the honest limits

- **Not a compliance validator.** It produces *audit-ready supporting evidence*; it does not confer compliance, certification, or a conformity assessment. Acceptance is the auditor's call.
- **Not a runtime.** No runtime logs. EU AI Act art. 12 record-keeping, Annex IV post-market and conformity-declaration items stay the **operator's** to supply. runward feeds the *design-time* technical documentation (art. 11 / Annex IV).
- **Point-in-time, and this claim has a history.** Evidence is derived from repository content; rerun it in CI. `runward check --freeze` seals it (SHA-256) and later drift reddens the gate until re-verified. That sentence was not true of every earlier build. ADR-0045 records an audit that sealed 31 files, rewrote every manifest row to `n/a`, and the seal still read intact: the seal covered the cited files, not the claim made about them. It covers both from 0.32.0. If you have a build older than 0.32.0 installed, the seal on this sheet describes a product you do not have.
- **No SLA, no warranty.** MIT "AS IS", best-effort, single maintainer. Forkability is the continuity guarantee.

### 5.1 What this gate has got wrong, and how it was found

On 2026-08-04, three adversarial self-audits run against the shipped binary, with every case executed rather than reasoned, made `runward check --strict` exit 0 on missions carrying no evidence at all. Twenty two false positives were proven, in nine defect classes. The cheapest cost 2 726 bytes of arbitrary text and no project code, with the seal applied and an ISO/IEC 42001 pack assembled on top. One class turned a symlink into an arbitrary file read; another made a signature run for over twenty seconds on thirty eight characters, which in CI means no verdict rendered at all.

The count is not the worst part. ADR-0045 records the aggravating property in its own words: the emptiest missions produced the most reassuring output, because the coverage counter printed only when something had been applied. A gate whose worst case is indistinguishable from its best, at the exit code and in the text, is not opposable. All nine classes are closed in 0.32.0; the full account is [ADR-0045](../adr/ADR-0045-the-gate-cannot-be-satisfied-by-paperwork.md), each item is listed with its affected version range in [known-defects.md](known-defects.md), and the campaign itself is replayable against the real CLI by anyone: `node test/audit-corpus.js`.

The asymmetry is what should decide how you deploy this: a false red costs an engineer an afternoon, a false green is what reaches an assessor.

### 5.2 How much this test suite detects, measured

On 2026-08-05 a full mutation pass was run against the seven library modules the verdict is computed from. **Mutation score 60.78 per cent**: 2 973 mutants, 1 769 killed, 38 timeout, 1 166 survived. Of the survivors, 433 carried mutators able to flip a decision and were re-run against the entire net, meaning the unit suite, the self-gate, the OSCAL schema validation and the end to end smoke; 53 died there and **380 survived everything**. Since that pass, 246 of the 380 have been instructed one by one and 181 now die, verified centrally against the augmented suite rather than claimed. The remaining 733 survivors were never confronted with the full net.

Read the perimeter with the score, because a score quoted without one is an overclaim. The measurement covers seven library modules. It does **not** cover `src/commands/check.ts`, which assembles the verdict and returns the exit code, and which no unit test imported until 2026-08-06. Since [ADR-0047](../adr/ADR-0047-the-verdict-is-computed-where-a-test-can-reach-it.md) the verdict itself is computed in `src/lib/verdict.ts`, inside the perimeter and at 97.79 per cent line and 100 per cent function coverage; what stays outside is the rendering. It does not cover the nine other commands, nor five `lib/` modules nothing reaches even transitively, nor the nine other modules outside the core, which include `src/lib/compliance.ts`, the module that emits the OSCAL pack described in section 6.

Three findings mattered, and they were all of the same kind: a mechanism that was behaving **correctly** and that **no test guarded**. Detection that a sealed mission has been altered, where one flipped field in `verifyEvidenceLock` turns a tampered sealed mission from exit 1 into exit 0, with the violations neither printed nor counted, because `src/commands/check.ts` gates the whole seal section on that field. The screen that rejects catastrophic rule signatures ([ADR-0020](../adr/ADR-0020-rule-evidence-signatures.md)), whose nested group collapse loop no fixture entered and which could be deleted with the suite still green. And containment of evidence pointers inside the repository, whose repository fallback was dead code under test. None of the three was an observed defect in a shipped release; all three were regression exposure. All three are pinned by tests from **0.33.0**, the first release to carry them: between 2026-08-05, when the pass found them, and that release, they were unguarded on every published version, and the register says so rather than back-dating them. If you have 0.32.0 or earlier installed, this paragraph describes a build you do not have. The full account is [ADR-0046](../adr/ADR-0046-mutation-testing-is-an-instrument-not-a-gate.md).

Say plainly what 60.78 per cent is. It is a measure of the detection power of this project's own net, on a named perimeter, at a date. It is not a quality rating and it is not reassurance. It bounds how much of your own tool assessment you can reasonably delegate to the supplier, which is exactly the use an assessor will put it to.

### 5.3 What is not measured, and therefore not claimed

- The path that produces the verdict was, until 2026-08-06, exercised end to end and by the self-gate and by no unit test: `src/commands/check.ts` sat at 8.70 per cent line and zero function coverage, and the mutation pass could not reach it. [ADR-0047](../adr/ADR-0047-the-verdict-is-computed-where-a-test-can-reach-it.md) moved the verdict into `src/lib/verdict.ts`, which a unit test imports directly and which measures 97.79 per cent line and 100 per cent function coverage; 24 golden outputs across four missions and six flag combinations were compared byte for byte to show the move changed nothing observable. What remains in `check.ts` is the rendering, and the coverage now recorded against it comes from tests driving the real CLI in a child process, not from a unit test importing it. Read the state of this bullet against the version you installed: it ships in 0.33.0 and is in no earlier build.
- The module that derives the OSCAL statuses in section 6, `src/lib/compliance.ts`, is outside the measured perimeter. What is verified there is form: byte identical output against a versioned fixture, schema validation, and ingestion by a third party tool. The detection power of the net over the derivation of `partial` versus `implemented` has not been measured.
- runward's own mission passes its own gate with 17 rows applied, 0 deviated and 19 n/a, and 15 of the 17 applied rows carry a pointer the gate opened. That is 15 rows mechanically verified out of 36. The project's real decision journal lives in `docs/adr/`, outside the directory the gate inspects, by a decision recorded in the mission itself.
- No external body has reviewed this project. The 2026-08-04 and 2026-08-05 campaigns were run by the maintainer.
- Since 2026-08-11 the gate's requirements are stated one at a time, with the test that exercises each, in [tool-operational-requirements.md](tool-operational-requirements.md) (51 requirements). Read what it refuses to be before what it is: it is **not a qualification kit**, because a vendor kit's documents are produced under a quality system a third party has assessed and these are not, and its traceability guard checks that a citation **resolves**, never that the cited test is relevant. Section 10 of that document names what carries no requirement at all, including the compliance-pack derivation.
- Line coverage is **measured on every pull request** since 2026-08-09 and held above a committed floor, so it cannot quietly drop. It is a ratchet, not a target: the floor moves only by a deliberate commit, because a floor chased upward invites tests that assert nothing. Coverage says which lines ran, never whether a wrong answer would have been caught, which is what section 5.2 measures and what does not flatter.

### 5.4 Why runward issues no attestation about your application

The question comes up, and the answer is no, in any form: no badge, no signed statement, nothing runward emits about a third party's software.

**The reason is measured, not principled.** The gate's own machine contract cannot distinguish a substantial mission from one carrying no project code at all: both answer `verdict: "clean"`, `exitCode: 0`. The counters added in 0.33.2 make the two legible to a careful reader, but the verdict is the same, by design — the gate accepts prose where nothing can be pointed at, and says so per row (`N row(s) are prose: accepted on your judgment, never verified`, ADR-0004). A document built on that verdict would designate a folder the audited party writes, not an application.

Three things make it worse rather than better. The corpus the gate judges against belongs to the audited party ([ADR-0045](../adr/ADR-0045-the-gate-cannot-be-satisfied-by-paperwork.md)). The seal date is the mission's word (RWD-2026-0022). And issuer, subject and verifier would be the same party, which does not make an attestation hard, it makes it a contradiction in terms.

**What can be published instead is a replayable record, by you, in your repository.** Not something runward emits about you:

- the commit SHA, and the runward version pinned (never `latest`);
- the exact command: `npx runward@<version> check --strict --json`;
- the complete output, never a summary, never a boolean, never a badge;
- `GATE_NON_SCOPE` in the body of the text, not in a footnote;
- and the operative sentence: *replay it yourself*.

A reader can then verify at four levels, and only one of them checks content: a signature says which workflow ran; `npm audit signatures` says the verdict came from the published package rather than a fork; **replaying at the commit** is the only step that checks anything about your evidence; and reading the pointers by hand is the only step that judges whether they mean anything, which nothing automates, by decision. On a private repository the third level is unavailable to an outside reader, and most applications live in private repositories. A record that does not say so misleads by default in the commonest case.

The sentence never to write is *"this application was built with runward"*. It asserts something about a human process the gate sees no trace of. The admissible form names what happened and invites the reader to repeat it.

## 6. The OSCAL evidence pack

`runward compliance <regime>` emits an **OSCAL 1.2.2 component-definition** with SHA-256-deterministic UUIDs, byte-identical across runs, schema-validated and golden-tested in CI. It is **proven ingestible by a real, independent OSCAL tool**: on every CI run, **IBM compliance-trestle** (a compliance toolkit used in FedRAMP/NIST workflows, its models generated from the NIST metaschemas) loads and validates the pack (`test/oscal-ingest.py`). **End-to-end ingestion into your specific GRC SaaS (RegScale, Paramify, Xacta) is your verification step**, not a runward guarantee — see [oscal-ingest.md](oscal-ingest.md). runward maps a *traced engineering decision* to an OSCAL requirement; a rule mapped but not justified yields `partial`, never `implemented`.

One limit belongs here rather than in a footnote: the module that derives those statuses is outside the perimeter of the mutation measurement reported in section 5.2. What CI proves about this pack is that it is byte stable, schema valid, and ingestible by an independent tool. It does not prove that the status derivation is guarded against regression, because that has not been measured.

## 7. Execution traces: fenced by decision, not by promise

A field survey of agent harnesses ("Code as Agent Harness", arXiv:2605.18747 — a preprint, cited here as a neutral source documenting current practice, not as a claim about runward) observes, in its section "The Harness as a Distillation Surface", that "production harnesses are no longer only deployment infrastructure; they are becoming a dominant source of training data for the next generation of code-assistant models": vendors collect real usage traces from deployed harnesses and feed them back into model training.

For a regulated buyer the relevant question is not whether such collection is disclosed, but whether the tool *could* do it. runward is fenced against that usage by dated architecture decisions, not by a privacy promise:

- **No exporter exists to repurpose.** [ADR-0011](../adr/ADR-0011-neutral-ecosystem-standards-as-versioned-ports.md): runward "never implements the standard, exports the telemetry, provides the runtime, or ships the sandbox/memory". There is no telemetry path to quietly widen.
- **"Local with no data flow" is a recorded decision with a self-destruct clause.** [ADR-0031](../adr/ADR-0031-sovereign-engineering-evidence-for-regulated-environments.md) states the posture and its reopen condition: if runward ever introduces a data flow, telemetry, or a hosted surface, the ADR is void and the entire SaaS due-diligence set of section 1 reapplies. The fence names its own failure mode.
- **The raw material of distillation is out of scope by decision.** What a distillation surface needs is the operator's behaviour — harness transcripts, usage traces. [ADR-0039](../adr/ADR-0039-the-operator-layer-stays-outside-the-cli.md) keeps that object out of the MIT CLI ("Never in the MIT CLI: adoption audits over harness transcripts, operator-side cost/telemetry tooling"): runward reads the mission repo, nothing else.

What leaves runward is the **evidence bundle** — typed evidence pointers → sealed manifest → OSCAL component-definition (section 6) — written into your own repository and moving only when you move it. A promise can be softened in a release note; each fence above is a decision that must be explicitly superseded in writing, and the structural backstop is already in CI: the offline job runs network-isolated (section 2), and the drift guard below reddens if the posture changes before this page does.

## 8. Tool confidence: the analysis, adverse case first

If your sector classifies development tools before use, someone in your organisation has to produce that classification. This section is written to make that exercise fast by handing over the arguments against runward already instructed, with sources, so you are not discovering them mid assessment. It asserts no level and no class for runward, in any scheme. That determination is made in your environment, against your declared use of the tool, and no body has assessed this project.

### 8.1 The argument that raises the bar, stated first

Every scheme in this section asks a version of the same question: can a malfunction of this tool introduce or **fail to detect** an error, and would you notice. runward exists to detect a missing proof, section 5.1 records twenty two dated cases where it failed to, and section 5.2 records that until 2026-08-05 the mechanism detecting an altered seal was held by no test. Those are inputs that push a tool confidence analysis **upward**, meaning more work demanded of the tool and of you, not less. We publish them because an assessor will find them and because a supplier who withholds an unfavourable number he holds is worth less to an assessment than one who does not. Nobody should adopt runward believing that our disclosure makes the analysis lighter.

One structural point cuts the other way, and it is yours to make, not ours: what your scheme weighs is your process, not our CLI. A team that keeps its human check and uses the gate to make that check cheaper is describing a different architecture from a team that deletes the human check because the gate is green. `GATE_NON_SCOPE`, printed in **every emitted pack** — the ISO/IEC 42001, NIST AI RMF and EU AI Act readiness drafts and the OSCAL component-definition (`src/lib/compliance.ts`) — and readable by machine in `runward rules --json`, states that a green row never proves the evidence implements the rule, that the gate reads bytes at rest and executes nothing, and that a green row does not travel forward in time. This page has now corrected itself twice, in opposite directions, on that one sentence: an early version claimed every pack carried it before that was true; the correction then claimed three packs did not, and stayed in print after 0.33.2 had made all four carry it (caught by the 2026-08-14 audit). The claim is therefore no longer maintained by hand: a blocking test asserts every emitted pack carries the non-scope ("every pack carries the declared non-scope, not just the one that stays home", `test/unit/compliance.test.js`), and this page follows the test. Which of those two architectures you build is an input to your analysis; the level that results belongs to you.

### 8.2 Medical device software

**IEC 62304 contains no tool validation requirement, and that is a delegation, not an exemption.** The edition in force is IEC 62304:2006/AMD1:2015, consolidated Edition 1.1 of June 2015. Every normative mention of tools in Edition 1.1 asks two things. Clause 5.1.4 requires the manufacturer to "include or reference in the software development plan: a) standards, b) methods, and c) tools associated with the development of SOFTWARE ITEMS of class C", Class C only. Clause 5.1.10 requires that "the items to be controlled shall include tools, items or settings, used to develop the MEDICAL DEVICE SOFTWARE, which could impact the MEDICAL DEVICE SOFTWARE", Class B and Class C, with compiler versions and environment settings as the given examples; clause 5.1.11 places those items under documented configuration management before verification. Identify, and configuration control. Not validate.

The standard explains the omission. Clause 4.1 requires a quality management system without writing one, and the informative Annex B.4.1 gives the reason: organisational processes "have been omitted from this standard. These PROCESSES are covered by a quality management system", naming ISO 13485. The informative Annex C, Table C.3, maps IEC 60601-1 clause 14.6.2, which asks that "suitably validated tools and PROCEDURES shall be selected and identified to implement each RISK CONTROL measure", onto clause 5.1.4, with the comment that "this standard requires the identification of specific tools and methods to be used for development in general, not for each RISK CONTROL measure". So if your device is a programmable electrical medical system in the scope of IEC 60601-1 clause 14, that clause asks for validated tools where a tool implements a risk control measure, and the silence of IEC 62304 is not an answer to it. Note the edition: Table C.3 of IEC 62304 Edition 1.1 maps to IEC 60601-1 Edition 3.1; the edition in force in 2026 is 3.2, and whether AMD2:2020 changed 14.6.2 is not established here. The IEC 62304 clauses quoted above were read in a consolidated copy found online rather than a licensed IEC copy; re-read them before relying on them.

**The obligation lives in ISO 13485:2016 clause 4.1.6**, which requires documented procedures for validating the application of computer software used in the quality management system, validation before initial use and, as appropriate, after changes to the software or its application, with the approach proportionate to the risk associated with the use of the software, and records kept. That is a paraphrase, not a quotation: the official ISO copy has not been read for this page, and a widely circulated verbatim of this clause splices in wording that belongs to clauses 7.5.6 and 7.6. Do not quote 4.1.6 from a blog, including this one. What matters for you is the object: what gets validated is the *application* of the software in your process, which is why nothing on this page can validate anything on your behalf. What runward can supply are the inputs: a declared intended use, a declared non scope readable by machine, version identity, deterministic behaviour, a defect register, and a measured detection figure.

Whether a development tool falls under 4.1.6 at all is a reading, not a clause. The dominant reading is that design and development is a process of the quality management system, so software that automates a design control decision is in scope. No sentence of ISO 13485 names development tools. If your notified body reads it differently, theirs is the reading that counts.

In the United States, since 2 February 2026, this is federal law rather than a standard: 21 CFR 820.7(b) incorporates ISO 13485:2016 by reference and 820.10(a) requires a conforming quality management system. The final rule is Federal Register document 2024-01709, published 2 February 2024, effective 2 February 2026, with technical amendments in document 2025-21955, published 4 December 2025. The old 21 CFR 820.70(i) went with the rewrite of part 820; its requirements now sit across ISO 13485 clauses 4.1.6, 7.5.6 and 7.6, and the rule text consulted does not publish a clause by clause mapping, so treat that correspondence as a reading. A page written before February 2026 will cite 820.70(i); that is the tell that it has not been maintained.

FDA's **Computer Software Assurance for Production and Quality System Software** guidance is final: docket FDA-2022-D-0795, Federal Register notice 2025-18468 of 24 September 2025, superseding section 6 of the General Principles of Software Validation. Its scope is computers and automated data processing systems used as part of medical device production or the quality system. What CSA changes is how much rigour you apply and how much you record, not whether you assess. Beyond the title, docket, dates, scope and supersession, this page states nothing about the guidance text, which has not been obtained.

One point of scope in runward's favour, with its condition attached: runward is not SOUP. Definition 3.29 covers a software item incorporated into the medical device; runward is never shipped in the device and does not generate the device code. Its writes go to `runward/`, to generated harness artifacts, and to paths you explicitly request, and none of that enters the device software architecture. That removes the whole SOUP regime, including the published anomaly list review of clause 7.1.3, and it holds only for as long as that sentence stays true.

### 8.3 Automotive and rail

**ISO 26262-8:2018 clause 11 is triggered by how you consume the output, not by what kind of tool it is.** The clause applies where the safety lifecycle uses a software tool such that activities or tasks required by the standard rely on its correct functioning, **and** where the relevant outputs of that tool are not examined or verified for the applicable process step. Both limbs must hold. The second one is squarely met by the way this tool is sold: `runward/contracts/port-contract.md` describes its exit codes as consumed blind by CIs, with the reaction on exit 0 being to cross the gate and merge. Whether the first limb holds depends on whether a task required by the standard rests on the verdict in your process.

**Tool impact** is defined as the possibility that a malfunction of the tool can introduce or fail to detect errors in a safety related item or element being developed, with the lower class available only where an argument shows no such possibility exists. Section 5.1 of this page is twenty two dated instances of failing to detect. That argument is not available to us, and verification tool vendors classify their own suites the same way for the same reason.

**Tool error detection** is confidence in measures that prevent the tool from malfunctioning and producing erroneous output, or that detect that it has malfunctioned, with the highest class requiring a high degree of confidence that a malfunction and its erroneous output will be prevented or detected. Section 5.2 documents three such measures that were correct and guarded by no test until 2026-08-05. That is material against a high detection class based on the tool alone. Detection can also be argued from *your* measures, a review of the report, an independent second check, a re-derivation, and that argument is available to you and not to us.

There is also a requirement addressing tools used to tailor the development process such that activities or tasks required by the standard are omitted, which forbids the **middle** detection class in that case. Two cautions, both of which matter if you are going to use this. First, it excludes the middle class only: the highest and the lowest both remain open, so it does not on its own produce the most demanding confidence level, and a page telling you otherwise has made a logical jump. Second, the exact subclause number in the 2018 edition is not established here; the widely reproduced number belongs to the 2011 first edition, and clause 11.4.5 of the 2018 edition has four subclauses, not five. The clause wording used above comes from a third party tool qualification plan reproducing the standard, not from a licensed copy. Read clause 11.4.5 in a licensed 2018 copy before you write a number in your plan.

**What that costs, realistically.** Of the four qualification methods, increased confidence from use is weak for this project: thirty eight tags in a month, ten minor versions in the five days to 2026-08-04, a 0.y.z public interface, and a release that deliberately turns previously green missions red. Evaluation of the tool development process is not structurally closed to a single maintainer project, but in practice the industry route runs through a certifying body, and there is no organisation here to evaluate. Development in accordance with a safety standard does not apply. That leaves validation of the software tool, and it is the one where this project has material to give: an adversarial audit corpus, a defect register, and a documented examination of behaviour under anomalous conditions such as catastrophic signatures, symlinks and malformed pointers. Note that this is also the method a supplier can support without being an organisation, which is precisely why it is where our effort goes.

**Rail.** The current reference is **EN 50716:2023**, published by CENELEC on 17 November 2023, superseding EN 50128:2011 with its corrigendum and amendments and EN 50657:2017 with its amendment. It keeps clause 6.7 and the T1/T2/T3 classes. Cite it as the current reference and EN 50128:2011 as superseded; note also that the transition is not finished and that projects launched under EN 50128:2011 legitimately still work to it. On the classes: T3 covers tools generating output that contributes to the executable code, and runward does not, so T3 is not the question. Between T1 and T2, the T2 definition in EN 50128:2011, a tool supporting test or verification where "errors in the tool can fail to reveal defects but cannot directly create errors in the executable software", is a literal description of what section 5.1 records. Argue T1 only if the verdict decides nothing in your process. Clause 6.7 requires the selection of T2 and T3 tools to be justified, with the justification identifying potential failures that can be injected into the tool's output and the measures to avoid or handle them; it requires a specification or manual defining the behaviour of the tool and any constraints on its use; and it requires configuration management to keep tool versions justified, with re-justification on change, which may in defined conditions rely on evidence provided for an earlier version. The subclause numbering in EN 50716:2023 is not established here, and at least one requirement was reworded between editions.

The same obligation exists at the root: IEC 61508-3:2010 clause 7.4.4.5 requires an assessment of offline support tools in classes T2 and T3 to determine the level of reliance placed on them and their potential failure mechanisms, with mitigation where such mechanisms are identified, and clause 7.4.4.4 requires a specification or product documentation defining the behaviour of the tool and constraints on its use. Class definitions are in IEC 61508-4:2010 clause 3.2.11.

One consequence worth stating plainly, because it is why the register in section 5 exists at all: a single register of known defects and constraints serves the ISO 26262 requirement for a description of known malfunctions and safeguards, the EN 50716 requirement to identify injectable failures, and IEC 61508-3 clause 7.4.4.5. It is written once. What it cannot do is discharge the requirement for you: `runward/contracts/port-contract.md` and `GATE_NON_SCOPE` are material you cite while writing your own justification, and section 5.1 documents a period during which that material was incomplete, because nine defect classes were in no ADR, no template, and not in `GATE_NON_SCOPE`.

### 8.4 Airborne software

The editions recognised by FAA Advisory Circular AC 20-115D of 21 July 2017, which cancels AC 20-115C, are RTCA DO-178C of 13 December 2011 (EUROCAE ED-12C, January 2012) and RTCA DO-330, *Software Tool Qualification Considerations*, of 13 December 2011 (ED-215, January 2012). Paragraph 10 of that AC states that section 12.2 of DO-178C and DO-330 "provide an acceptable method for tool qualification", and that DO-330 "contains its own complete set of objectives, activities, and life cycle data for tool qualification". The same AC records, as of 2017, that EASA AMC 20-115D is likewise based on ED-12C. Whether a later revision of any of these exists in 2026 is not established here; the FAA Dynamic Regulatory System entry for AC 20-115 and the current EASA ED Decision are where to check. The DO-178C and DO-330 primary texts were not consulted for this page; the statements below come from secondary sources and are reported as such.

**The trigger, and the cheapest correct posture.** Qualification arises when a process of the standard is automated, eliminated or reduced and the tool's output is not itself verified; AC 20-115D paragraph 10.a directs applicants to the criteria of DO-178C section 12.2 to determine whether qualification is needed. If you run runward as an engineering discipline and claim no DO-178C Annex A objective credit on the strength of its verdict, no tool qualification arises. That is the correct and cheapest posture, and it belongs in your plans rather than left implicit, because the opposite happens by drift: the first time a review is shortened because the gate was green, the trigger has been pulled and nobody wrote it down.

**Where the criteria actually divide.** DO-178C section 12.2 distinguishes a tool that could fail to detect an error from one that automates verification, could fail to detect an error, **and** whose output is used to justify eliminating or reducing verification processes *other than those automated by the tool*, or development processes that could affect the airborne software. That qualifier is the whole distinction, and it is easy to get backwards. Using the gate to shorten the documentary completeness check that the gate itself performs stays on the first side of the line. Using a green gate to justify shortening a code review, a test campaign or an analysis the gate never performed crosses to the second. Per the level table in that section, the first case lands on the least demanding qualification level at every software level; the second lands one level more demanding at software levels A and B, and on the least demanding level at C and D. The economic consequence sits entirely in that step: the least demanding level draws on fourteen objectives, all from the tool operational, configuration management, quality assurance and qualification liaison tables, and asks about the *use* of the tool. The next level up adds twenty four objectives, twenty of them from the tool's own planning, requirements, design, coding, integration and test tables, for a cumulative thirty eight. A NASA contractor report puts the consequence bluntly: where a tool was built by a third party, that level and above are not achievable without artifacts provided by the tool developer, which for a tool whose author never produced them may be difficult or impossible to obtain. runward has none of those artifacts. Which side of the criterion line your declared use sits on is therefore not a formality here; it decides whether a path exists at all.

**What runward already has, under other names.** `runward/contracts/port-contract.md` carries command signatures, the exit code contract, output schemas and operational properties including read only operation on your code, determinism, no model and no network. A unit suite of 342 tests in 38 files at 0.33.0, against 196 in 22 files at 0.32.0; an end to end smoke run; OSCAL schema validation and golden tests; the self-gate; and an adversarial audit corpus. Both figures are given because they differ, and because a qualification argument is written against a version rather than against a branch. A committed lockfile, SHA pinned CI actions, a CycloneDX SBOM, SLSA provenance, release tags and a changelog. `GATE_NON_SCOPE`, machine readable through `runward rules --json`. **What it does not have:** any of the tool plans and standards, a numbered and individually verifiable set of tool requirements, a design description, trace data linking requirement to test case, or an assembled configuration index. Those gaps are named so you can price them rather than discover them. The applicant side of the work, the plans, the tool operational requirements, the installation report and the operational verification, is yours in every case and runward will never claim to supply it.

**The fallback is weak here, and structurally so.** Where a tool's development data does not exist, service history is the usual route, and it depends among other things on stability, maturity, and the availability of problem reporting. This project is at 0.33.5, before 1.0, with a recent release that deliberately reddens previously green missions, so history accumulated on earlier versions says little about the current one. Problem reporting exists as a channel and, until [known-defects.md](known-defects.md), did not exist as a register. The faster and harder this project corrects itself, the less its service history carries. That is a real cost of the way it is built, and it is stated rather than hidden.

One measured point in the other direction, from the same pass that produced the unfavourable numbers: `check.ts` imports neither the territory module nor characterize, and 42 territory mutants applied one at a time to a green mission left `check --strict` at exit 0 every time, while 4 of them already corrupted the output of `runward rules --for --json`. That is evidence of partition rather than an assertion of partition, which is the form these schemes ask for. Publish it with its warning: a partition that protects the verdict while letting the machine contract answer a wrong list, plausibly, is half a partition.

### 8.5 The ceiling on our own wording

The strongest sentence this project is entitled to write is the one MathWorks writes about its own kits: the material lets **you** qualify runward **in your environment**. The same vendor states the limit that makes the sentence credible, that its kit "does not 'prequalify' tools" and that "tool qualification must be performed in the context of each specific project and operational environment", with the tool user ultimately responsible for showing the tool is adequate for its intended use and verifying its operation in the installed environment. LDRA writes at the same height, offering "artefacts and guidance to simplify the process of qualifying" its tools "in a project-specific environment". The rung above that, where a vendor can say no further qualification is needed, is derived entirely from a certificate issued by a certification body on a named product against named standards. There is no body here, and without one that sentence is not ambitious, it is empty.

The default frame a reviewer applies to an open source verification tool is already written, by a competitor selling against them: open source coverage tools "lack certification or qualification for use as verification tools in safety-critical software development" adhering to ISO 26262, IEC 61508, IEC 62304, EN 50128 and DO-178C, because their authors do not serve that market. Substitute runward and the sentence holds. We cite it here rather than let it be said first, and we answer it with what we have: the register, the corpus, and the measured number. Section 8.3 notes that these schemes ask a description of known malfunctions of any supplier, so this is the expected artifact, not a distinguishing one. It is a disclosure claim, not a confidence claim, and the two are not interchangeable. Two things stand between this project and the rung above, and only one of them is administrative: there is no legal entity to be the applicant, and none of the tool life cycle data a certification body would examine exists, as the list in section 8.4 says. Closing the second is years of work that has not started.
